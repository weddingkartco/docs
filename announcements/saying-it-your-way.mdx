title: "When a template won't fit your words"
description: Three ways to send exactly what you want to say, and how to pick between them
---

Ready-made announcements are quick because the wording is already approved by WhatsApp. That is also their limit: you can fill in the blanks, but you cannot rewrite the message.

Most people hit this wall the same way — a last-minute change, a long notice, a list of instructions that simply does not fit. You have two ways out, and both are already in the app.

## Pick your route

| | Ready-made message | Ready-made + your own image | Write your own message |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Your exact words** | No — the wording is fixed | Yes, inside the image | Yes |
| **Reaches every guest right away** | Yes | Yes | No — waits until the guest opens the chat |
| **Best for** | Invitations, RSVPs, save-the-dates | Long notices, schedules, menus, travel and room details | Short day-of notes to guests already chatting with you |

<Note>
If everyone must receive it today, use one of the first two. **Write your own message** is the only route where some guests may never receive it.
</Note>

While editing a ready-made announcement, the **Need to say more than this?** card links straight to these routes (**Attach an image** when the template accepts one, and **Write your own message**).

## Put your words on an image

This is the route most people miss, and it is usually the right one. You get to say anything you like, and it still reaches every guest immediately.

It works because the announcement itself stays a short approved line — everything you actually want to say travels inside the image attached to it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make the image">
    Use anything you like — ChatGPT, Canva, or even a clear photo of a printed notice. Put your full text on it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start a ready-made announcement">
    Tap **Add Announcement** (the **+** button), then **Send a ready-made message**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick an announcement that accepts a photo">
    In the editor, the **Add Attachment** area lists the formats that template allows. If **Image** is greyed out, go back and pick a different template.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach your image">
    Tap **Upload** and choose the image you made from your photo library or files.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep the caption simple">
    The template's own short approved line travels as the caption. Choose one whose wording works as a pointer — something like "Details for tomorrow — please see the image" is enough.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Making an image guests can actually read

WhatsApp compresses every image before it reaches a guest, so fine print does not survive the trip.

- **Big text, few words.** Around 40 words is the practical ceiling.
- **Strong contrast.** Dark text on a light background. Gold on cream looks lovely on a laptop and disappears on a phone.
- **Portrait, roughly 1080 × 1350.** Fills the screen without being shrunk further.
- **Never put a phone number or link only in the image.** Nothing inside an image is tappable — if the template has a blank for it, repeat it in the message too.
- **Put the headline in the message too, where the template allows.** Some guests never open images.

## Write your own message

Best for quick notes during the wedding days, to guests who are already in conversation with you.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a new announcement">
    Tap **Add Announcement** (the **+** button).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Write your own message">
    Under **On WhatsApp**, pick **Write your own message**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type it">
    No template, no approval, no blanks to fill. Say it however you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach something if you need to">
    Free-form messages accept an image, a video or a PDF. You can add reply buttons instead, but not both — WhatsApp cannot send an attachment and reply buttons in the same message.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send">
    Guests with an open chat receive it straight away. Everyone else gets a short opener first, and your message follows once they reply.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Guests who never reply to the opener never receive the message (you get the credit back for any message that is never delivered). On guest lists with many elderly or VIP guests, expect only a minority to respond. For anything everyone must know, send a ready-made message with your image attached instead.
</Warning>

## Let the app make the picture

In the announcement editor, tap **Browse Designs** under **Add Attachment** to start from a ready-made design with your wedding details already applied. You can then describe changes and the app redraws it. Images you made earlier are under **Saved**.

Use it when you want something that *looks* good and the exact wording does not matter much.

<Warning>
Do not use it to carry exact details. AI image generation rewrites, misspells and drops words, and it is markedly less reliable in Hindi than in English. If timings, room numbers or names have to be correct, make the image yourself and use **Upload Attachment**, or use the exact-text card described below.
</Warning>

## Turn your text into a card

If you just want your words on a clean background — no design work, no AI rewriting them — the app can draw the card for you. What you type is exactly what guests read.

Because the text stays large enough to read on a phone, there is a limit to how much fits on one card. If your message is too long, the app tells you roughly how much to trim rather than producing something unreadable.

<Note>
Hindi text needs extra font support on the server. If it is unavailable, the app will say so and ask you to make the image yourself instead of producing a card with broken characters.
</Note>
